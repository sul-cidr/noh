Index of Intermedia

The Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this second dan of the second act—which comprises a single shōdan—is lower than that of the preceding shōdan.

This shōdan features entrance music that marks the shite’s return to the stage, performed by the three drums in a strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. Its IoI is the lowest in the second act.


This dynamic entrance music announces the arrival of a powerful character; in Takasago, it heralds the God of Sumiyoshi. To that end it deploys the full and rhythmically charged hayashi: the non-congruent nohkan is accompanied by the three percussion instruments in a strict rhythmic setting. The impact is all the stronger because this is the first shōdan of the second act to include the full hayashi, and the taiko's first appearance in the play. Consequently, the Index of Intermedia rises as the shōdan continues to elevate the play's energy level.

The taiko part unfolds in five cycles of patterns that follow the standard sequence: Head pattern → 1st transition → Ground pattern → 2nd transition → Closing pattern. The Head pattern, kashira, articulates the beginning of each cycle; it is played seven, five, three, two, and finally one time before the remainder of each cycle proceeds. This decreasing number of kashira patterns creates a formal accelerando, emphasized by the steady increase in tempo throughout the shōdan. As this particular performance of Takasago was staged to celebrate an auspicious occasion, the festive yang numbers—7, 5, 3, and 1—are prominently featured in the repetitions.

The nohkan makes two entries in the shōdan. The first, over the taiko's opening ground pattern, frames the beginning of the shōdan; the second, over the penultimate one, underlines the nochi-shite's entrance onto the bridge. His arrival is imbued with the rising energy of the music: fast and dynamic. As the curtain rises, he immediately turns to face front, performing the dynamic Figure Eight kata that showcases his divine stature, a slight slowing of the music giving it a mark. He and the music accelerate again as he proceeds to the First Pine, where he turns to face front and executes the standard sequence of kata: Forward Point and Open-retreat, again with a slight slowdown of the music underlining the arrival gestures.
