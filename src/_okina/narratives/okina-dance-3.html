Form
Okina-Okina performs:
Dance-1 → Dance-2 → Dance-3
Synopsis
This is Okina’s last prayer calling for thousands of peace and prosperity.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The Dance-3 has a cadential feeling since Okina performs all his kata upstage, on Square 8.

Okina’s dance is structured in three sections, the first and third of which incorporate dance with accompanying text, while the second, and most extended section, is an instrumental dance.

Okina’s Dance-3, with its static quality, presents itself as an extended cadential section for the three-part dance. The instrumental component is confined to the three kotsuzumi participating in the performance of a one-honji pattern that emphasizes beats 1, 3, 5, and 7, a pattern initially introduced in Okina’s Song-1:

All strokes are preceded by a kakegoe, with the exception of the stroke on the seventh beat, which is preceded by a pick-up stroke on beat 6.5.

The dance's kata is limited in both number and spatial dimension, as they are all executed upstage, specifically at Square 8. Okina concludes this concise dance in a manner analogous to the commencement of the first, with closed raised arms and his face concealed by his fan.

Finally, the text exhibits a repetitive structure, primarily focusing on a single word: Manzairaku, sung in alternation between Okina and the jiutai. In Japanese, Manzairaku signifies "eternal happiness" or "music for ten thousand years," and it is associated with auspicious and joyful commencements. Within the context of Gagaku music, it denotes a graceful and ceremonial dance, performed on celebratory occasions such as imperial accession ceremonies, to express wishes for a long-lasting and prosperous dynasty. According to legend, a phoenix's cry inspired the song, which was believed to bestow "ten thousand years" upon a wise ruler. In the context of Takasago, it reinforces the term's association with longevity and celebratory events.
