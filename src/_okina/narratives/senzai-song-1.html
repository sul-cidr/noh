Form
Okina-Senzai performs:
Song-1 → Dance-1 → Song-2 → Dance-2
Synopsis
Senzai Song-1 is the first work in the Section’s third subsection. Senzai is reminding us of nature’s unpredictable power and the presence of malevolent spirits.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in sashinori is sung in yowagin style by Senzai and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
Senzai positioned himself center stage to deliver his warnings of potential dangers to come.

This subsection is the first in a series of four focusing on Senzai, who is granted the honor of presenting the play's inaugural dance. These four subsections are presented in two pairs: the first of each pair is sung, while the second is danced.

Senzai functions as an attendant or servant to Okina. He is typically portrayed as a younger figure, representing the future generation that will inherit the traditions and wisdom embodied by Okina. Senzai's dance and songs are characterized by youthful energy and exuberance, bringing a sense of lightheartedness and celebration to the performance.

The text of Senzai's initial song, delivered in alternation with the jiutai, centers on the imagery of booming water in the autumn. In traditional Japanese culture, the sound of booming or roaring water symbolizes the overwhelming and unpredictable power of nature, and the potential presence of dangerous spirits. In contrast to serene water, which represents purification, booming water often signifies chaos, peril, and the uncontrollable forces of the natural world.

While singing, Senzai stands and commences his dance, focusing his assertive kata on center stage, moving between Square 8 and the two most dynamic squares: 9 and 4.

The momentum generated by this engaging dance is further underscored by the three kotsuzumi, which maintain a repeating three-beat pattern composed of two strokes: one beat with the 'chi' sound, and two beats with the 'po' sound.
