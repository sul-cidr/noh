Form
Okina-Senzai Performs:
Senzai Song 1 → Senzai-no-mai 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai-no-mai 2
Synopsis
Through song and assertive kata, Senzai introduces the theme of nature's formidable power, centered on the metaphor of booming water. This imagery is established in alternation by both Senzai and the jiutai.
Text
Regular. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Senzai and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The visual focus of this shōdan is Senzai’s dance. He performs assertive and energetic kata, concentrating his movements along the center line of the stage. A recurring visual highlight is a mimetic kata where Senzai bends down and then rises onto his toes, a powerful gesture that physically represents the force and resilience of water.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this third dan, which comprises four shōdan, rises to the first act’s highest level in a sawtooth pattern.

The first shōdan features an arhythmic song performed by Senzai and the jiutai, supported by three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. It is the play’s first dance-to-text as it also includes stage movements. This combination helps raise the IoI to a high mid-range level.


This dance-to-text song rises the Index of Intermedia (IoI) by adding dance to the interplay of vocal, nohkan and percussion parts. The text, delivered in alternation by Senzai and the jiutai, centers on the imagery of booming water, a symbol of resilience and nature’s powerful forces.

The momentum is driven by the three kotsuzumi drums, which maintain a simple, constantly repeating triple rhythm of a short 'chi' followed by a long 'po' sound both preceded by a ho kakegoe. As he sings, Senzai performs assertive kata, focusing his movements along the center stage line. The most direct link between text and movement occurs when the lyrics mention booming water. At these moments, Senzai performs a mimetic kata, bending down before rising onto his toes to embody the power and resilience of water, and repeats the gesture when his own chanted lines refer to water flowing without end.
