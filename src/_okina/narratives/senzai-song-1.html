Form
Okina-Senzai performs:
Song-1 → Dance-1 → Song-2 → Dance-2
Synopsis
Senzai Song-1 is the first work in the Section’s third subsection. Senzai is reminding us of nature’s unpredictable power and the presence of malevolent spirits.
Text
Regular. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Senzai and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
Senzai positioned himself center stage to deliver his warnings of potential dangers to come.

This shōdan is the first in a series of four focusing on Senzai, who is honored with the play's first dance. These four shōdan are organized in two pairs, with each pair consisting of a sung part followed by a danced part. The songs have medium IoI while the dances high IoI.

Senzai, typically portrayed as a younger figure, serves as an attendant to Okina. His dance and songs are characterized by youthful energy and exuberance, bringing a sense of lightheartedness and celebration to the performance.

The text of Senzai's initial song, delivered in alternation with the jiutai, centers on the imagery of booming water. In Japanese culture, this sound symbolizes the overwhelming and uncontrollable forces of the natural world, sometimes signifying chaos, peril, and the presence of powerful spirits.

As he sings, Senzai begins his dance, focusing his assertive kata at center stage as he moves between Squares 8, 9, and 4. When the jiutai sings of booming water, Senzai, standing at the front of the stage on Square 4, performs what can be interpreted as a mimetic kata. He bends down before rising onto his toes, embodying the power and momentum of water 15’38”. He repeats this kata when his own lyrics refer to water flowing without end 15’56”.

The momentum of this engaging dance is further underscored by the three kotsuzumi drums. They maintain a simple, constantly repeating pattern of two alternating strokes: a short 'chi' followed by a long 'po' sound.
