Form
Okina-Introductory songs:
Song-1 → Song-2
Synopsis
Okina’s song-2 is the second work in the Section’s second subsection. The text sung by Okina-to-be and the jiutai is a repetition of the text from a lost language introduced in Song-1. In fact, Song-2 is a continuation of Song-1, that we labeled as Song-2 because the kotsuzumi’s rhythmic pattern is much more dynamic.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The song is delivered over a standstill stage, since Okina-to-be remains seated at Square 7 as he delivers his line.

Song-2 represents a continuation of Song-1; however, the supporting rhythmic pattern performed by the three kotsuzumi players differs significantly from the preceding two. Consequently, it is used to demarcate the beginning of Okina's Song-2. The three kotsuzumi execute a four-beat ostinato, repeated twelve times. This ostinato, characterized by alternating 'chi' and 'po' sounds on each beat, constitutes the most dynamic rhythmic pattern among the three employed in the two songs. The sequence of six honji commences at a metronomic tempo of ♩= 50, subsequently accelerating to ♩ = 112 by the fifth honji. A cadence is then signaled by a deceleration to ♩ = 66 at the sixth and final honji.
