Okina-Introductory songs:
Song-1 → Song-2
Synopsis
The musical ritual continues with Okina’s Song-2, also performed in alternation by the Okina-to-be and the jiutai. This song reiterates the onomatopoeic, ritualistic text of the first, but sets it against a dramatically different accompaniment. A simple, driving drum ostinato accelerates to an extreme tempo, creating a powerful sense of dynamic energy on an otherwise motionless stage and marking the climax of the introductory songs.
Text
The arhythmic text, set in sashinori, is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The song is delivered over a standstill stage with Okina-to-be remaining seated at Square 7.

Okina's Song-2 maintains the same Index of Intermedia as the core elements remain consistent: the stage is visually static, with the Okina-to-be singing from Square 7 in alternation with the jiutai, and the text reiterates the onomatopoeic, ritualistic quality of the first song. The crucial difference, and the source of the scene's new intensity, lies entirely in the musical accompaniment.

The three kotsuzumi drums establish a simple but driving ostinato (a repeating rhythmic pattern) that provides a transparent and coherent foundation for the vocals. The critical shift lies in the tempo: the drums undergo an extreme acceleration—more than doubling in speed—to generate immense energy on the motionless stage, creating the most dynamic experience of the performance so far. After decelerating to signal the song's cadence, the entire singing subsection is audibly framed. Just as it began with the flute's piercing Hishigi pattern, it concludes with another, sealing off this part of the ritual.

THIS BELOW IS A DETAILED MUSIC-ONLY DESCRIPTION

Song-2 represents a continuation of Song-1; however, the supporting rhythmic pattern performed by the three kotsuzumi players differs significantly from the preceding two. Consequently, it is used to demarcate the beginning of Okina's Song-2. The three kotsuzumi execute a four-beat ostinato, repeated twelve times. This ostinato, characterized by alternating 'chi' and 'po' sounds on each beat, constitutes the most dynamic rhythmic pattern among the three employed in the two songs.
