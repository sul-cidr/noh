Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the third and last dan of the second act consists of two shōdan, maintaining the IoI at its highest level for most of the section.

The first shōdan is an instrumental dance, accompanied by the ōtsuzumi and three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting, and a congruent nohkan part. As the last of five instrumental dances in Okina, it temporarily forgoes verbal expression in favor of choreographic refinement, thereby raising the IoI to its peak.


Following the quiet, unaccompanied dialogue of the Mondō, the final shōdan of the act, the Suzunodan, erupts. As a purely instrumental dance, it raises the Index of Intermedia to its absolute peak. This is not just a performance but a climactic ritual offering. The suzu (bell tree), symbolizing ears of rice, is the central object; its ringing is a sonic prayer for a bountiful harvest, purifying the space and inviting blessings from the divine.

The shōdan opens with a transitional musical section (Donato) as Senzai exits and Sanbasō prepares. He moves to the front and then back to the center, where he slows and leans forward, covering his masked face with a fully open fan. This creates a powerful image of layered mystery, a mask upon a mask, heightening anticipation. The dance proper begins with the piercing hishigi pattern of the nohkan. Initially, the tempo is slow and deliberate. The nohkan plays evenly spaced notes against which Sanbasō shakes the suzu on the offbeats, in perfect sync with the kotsuzumi drums. His movements are meticulous; he leans low, shaking the bell close to the stage as if sowing seeds directly into the earth.

As the music and dance begin a long, steady acceleration, the intermedia expression intensifies. Sanbasō rises, lifting the fan and suzu high above his head while introducing loud, resonant stamps. This marks the start of a new dynamic phase where he covers all corners of the stage, ensuring the purifying blessing of the bells reaches everywhere. The dance is built on this cycle of gathering and releasing power: after a period of high energy, Sanbasō returns to the low, forward-leaning posture, briefly containing the energy before rising again to release it with even greater force as the tempo continues to build.

The final section brings the ritual to its climax. Occupying the center line of the stage, the density of the performance grows to its peak; the stamping can occur on nearly every beat, creating an almost overwhelming feeling of kinetic power and sound. Sanbasō performs a series of powerful contrasts: he stamps vigorously while ringing the bell high and holding the fan low, then reverses the gesture. After a final circle with arms outstretched, he returns to the center for one last powerful sequence of stomps. The ritual concludes after a final circle and with two nohkan hishigi cutting through the texture the music begins a dramatic slowdown and thinning. The ōtsuzumi falls silent. To this sparse accompaniment, Sanbasō drops to his knees, stretches his arms out one last time, and brings the fan and bell together in a final, reverent bow, sealing the blessing.
