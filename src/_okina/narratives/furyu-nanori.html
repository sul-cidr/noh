Form
Furyū-Furyūsenzai Enters:
Watari byōshi → Noriji-1 → Nanori
Synopsis
In this brief, spoken shōdan, Furyūsenzai directly addresses the audience to explain the real-world occasion for this special performance: the commemoration of the relocation of Kyoto City University of Arts. Lasting just over half a minute, his direct address connects the symbolic world of the play to the immediate reality of the celebration, grounding the ancient ritual in the here and now.
Text
Spoken prose
Music
Unaccompanied vocal part delivered in kotoba.
Visuals
The visual aspect of this shōdan is entirely static. Furyūsenzai remains motionless at Square 1 and performs no kata or other significant gestures. This lack of movement focuses all attention on the actor's presence and the clarity of his spoken words.

The 'Furyūsenzai Enters' dan concludes with this final shōdan, which has a very low Index of Intermedia. All musical accompaniment ceases, leaving only the unaccompanied spoken voice. This stark drop in complexity serves a crucial function: it strips away the layers of ritual performance to deliver direct, clear information. The vocal delivery itself is not simple speech but the stylized, clear intonation of the kyōgen style. By creating a moment of pure, unadorned text, the performance bridges the gap between the ancient rite and the present reality of its audience.
