Form
Furyū-Furyūsenzai Enters:
Watari byōshi → Noriji-1 → Nanori
Synopsis
In this brief, spoken shōdan, Furyūsenzai directly addresses the audience to explain the real-world occasion for this special performance: the commemoration of the relocation of Kyoto City University of Arts. Lasting just over half a minute, his direct address connects the symbolic world of the play to the immediate reality of the celebration, grounding the ancient ritual in the here and now. His address concludes with a dramatic turn and transition to the next shōdan. Reacting to an otherworldly sound, he will turn from narrator of the present to an observer of the imminent mystery. By announcing that he will wait, he will position himself as our guide and herals of the arrival of the auspicious spirits.
Text
Spoken prose
Music
Unaccompanied vocal part delivered in kotoba.
Visuals
The visual aspect of this shōdan is almost entirely static, which focuses all attention on the clarity of the spoken words. Furyūsenzai remains motionless at Square 1 for the duration of his speech, performing a single, deliberate step forward only as he delivers his final line. This small but significant gesture concludes his direct address to the audience.

The 'Furyūsenzai Enters' dan concludes with this final shōdan, which has a very low Index of Intermedia. All musical accompaniment ceases, leaving only the unaccompanied spoken voice. This stark drop in complexity serves a crucial function: it strips away the layers of ritual performance to deliver direct, clear information. The vocal delivery itself is not simple speech but the stylized, clear intonation of the kyōgen style. By creating a moment of pure, unadorned text, the performance bridges the gap between the ancient rite and the present reality of its audience. The section is decisively framed by a single, short, high-pitched hishigi tone from the nohkan at 44’18”. This unexpected piercing sound acts as a powerful auditory catalyst and a narrative bridge to the next dan, heralding the appearance of auspicious spirits.
