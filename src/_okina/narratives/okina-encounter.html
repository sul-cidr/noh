Form
Okina (White Mask)-Encounter:
Okina Encounter
Synopsis
Following Senzai's energetic dances, the tempo slows for the ceremonial first encounter between Okina and Sanbasō. They do not speak directly; instead, Okina sings and the jiutai answers on behalf of Sanbasō, with text that simply acknowledges each other’s presence. The pivotal moment occurs when, prompted by the jiutai’s chant, both characters rise to their feet and take two steps toward each other. They then separate, with Okina’s movement to Square 8 demonstrating that the play's focus has now shifted fully to him.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, based on the work Saibara from the Gagaku repertoire is set in sashinori, sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi and ōtsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The scene begins with a calming moment as Senzai walks to rest at Square 5. The focus then shifts to Okina and Sanbasō, who turn to face each other. In a coordinated movement, they rise first to a knee and then to a standing position. They take two small steps toward one another before separating; Okina moves to Square 8 while Sanbasō moves upstage. The shōdan concludes with Okina performing a final gesture: he extends his arms, brings his hands together, and covers his face with his open fan, establishing a reflective pose.
Index of Intermedia

The Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this fourth dan—which consists of a single shōdan—descends to a low midrange level.

This shōdan marks the first appearance of the ōtsuzumi. It features an arhythmic song performed by Okina and the jiutai, accompanied by the ōtsuzumi, three kotsuzumi drums in strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. The section also signals a shift in focus from Senzai to Okina, and its lower IoI may be seen as reflecting the transitional function of this dan.


The Index of Intermedia retreats as the performance turns to narrative chant and action. The pivotal first Encounter brings together the play’s two central deities and a fundamental duality: Okina, a noble deity whose dignified presence invokes universal peace, and Sanbasō, a robust and earthbound deity who dances and prays for the land’s fertility. Some scholars see parallels between their meeting and erotic encounters found in fertility rituals. The intermedia here masterfully orchestrates the dynamic of this charged meeting, forming an energetic wave that begins with a deliberate downturn. Following Senzai’s dances, the tempo slows to a ceremonial pace with scarce drumming as Senzai walks and sits down at Square 5.

The energy begins to rise again as the nohkan enters with the slow, low-register Notare no ryō pattern. Simultaneously, the three kotsuzumi drums establish a steady two-honji rhythmic pattern, creating a regular pulse that propels Okina to sing. While the jiutai responds, the nohkan slowly fades away, momentarily lowering the momentum as Okina sets aside the mask box, preparing to stand up. The energy then surges as Okina sings again, the nohkan re-enters with strong, high-pitched pattern Shingon no te, and the drums accelerate. The jiutai takes over, maintaining the increased intensity. This musical swell supports the pivotal moment when Okina and Sanbasō rise to their feet and face each other. Their encounter is further amplified as they take two steps toward each other and the ōtsuzumi drum interjects powerful strokes, disrupting the kotsuzumi's steady pattern.

When the jiutai finishes the text, the characters separate, and the energy shifts down to reflective as Okina walks toward Square 8. He transitions to the next shōdan with the ten-no-tappai, or the "Heaven” kata, consisting of bringing his arms together to cover the face with an open fan. This is the first of three such kata named after the fundamental trinity of Heaven, Earth, and Man. Crucially, this physical gesture is linked to a vocal shift: the singing style changes from the strong tsuyogin to the more melodious yowagin. This unique combination of reflective movement and a softer vocal tone establishes the perfect mood for the subsequent prayer.
