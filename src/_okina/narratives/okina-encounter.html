Form
Okina-Encounter:
Encounter
Synopsis
Following Senzai's energetic dances, the tempo slows for the ceremonial first encounter between Okina and Sanbasō-to-be. They do not speak directly; instead, Okina sings and the jiutai answers on behalf of Sanbasō-to-be, with text that simply acknowledges each other’s presence. The pivotal moment occurs when, prompted by the jiutai’s song, both characters rise to their feet and take two steps toward each other. They then separate, with Okina’s movement to Square 8 demonstrating that the play's focus has now shifted fully to him.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, based on the work Saibara from the Gagaku repertoire is set in sashinori, sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The scene begins with a calming moment as Senzai walks to rest at Square 5. The focus then shifts to Okina and Sanbasō-to-be, who turn to face each other. In a coordinated movement, they rise first to a knee and then to a standing position. They take two small steps toward one another before separating; Okina moves to Square 8 while Sanbasō-to-be moves upstage. The shōdan concludes with Okina performing a final gesture: he extends his arms, brings his hands together, and covers his face with his open fan, establishing a reflective pose.

This shōdan forms an energetic wave, beginning with a downturn. Following Senzai’s dynamic dances, the tempo slows to a ceremonial pace, and the scene opens with scarce drumming as Senzai walks and sits down at Square 5. The energy begins to rise again as the nohkan enters with the slow, low-register Notare no ryō pattern 19’25”. Simultaneously, the three kotsuzumi drums establish a steady one-honji rhythmic pattern, creating a regular pulse that propels Okina to sing. While the jiutai responds for Sanbasō-to-be, the nohkan slowly fades away, momentarily stalling the momentum. Okina sets aside the mask box, preparing to rise.

The energy then surges as Okina sings again, the nohkan re-enters with strong, high-pitched patterns, and the drums accelerate. The jiutai takes over, maintaining the increased intensity. This musical swell supports the pivotal moment when Okina and Sanbasō-to-be rise to their feet 20’41” and face each other. Their encounter is further amplified as they take two steps toward each other and the ōtsuzumi drum interjects powerful strokes, disrupting the kotsuzumi's steady pattern. When the jiutai finishes the text, the characters separate, and the energy shifts to reflective as Okina walks toward Square 8. He transitions to the next shōdan with the ten-no-tappai kata, or “paying respect to Heaven” 20’12”. This is the first of three such kata in the play honoring the fundamental trinity of Heaven, Earth, and Man. Crucially, this physical gesture is linked to a vocal shift: the singing style changes from the strong tsuyogin to the more melodious yowagin. This unique combination of reflective movement and a softer vocal tone establishes the perfect mood for the subsequent prayer.
