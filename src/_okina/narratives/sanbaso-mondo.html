Sanbasō-Encounter:
Mondō 2
Synopsis
Sanbasō calls for an Ado, a term used in Kyōgen to identify a contrasting character. Thus, Senzai appears and encourages Sanbasō to dance as a prayer for blessings to ten thousand years.

The dialogue is conducted with Sanbasō standing at Square 1, Senzai at Square 5.
Index of Intermedia

The Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this second dan of the second act, consists of a single shōdan, although it raises the IoI, it remains at a low level.

This shōdan features an unaccompanied dialogue between Sanbasō and Senzai.


Positioned at Square 1, Sanbasō alternates his gaze to the left and right, calling for an Ado. Senzai, located at Square 5, responds to this summons. They then engage in dialogue while remaining in their respective positions. At the dialogue's conclusion, Senzai presents a suzu bell to Sanbasō.

This suzu bell plays a significant role in the subsequent dance, where it is employed to symbolize ears of rice and invoke prayers for a bountiful harvest. Furthermore, the bell also holds broader significance in Shinto rituals, as it is believed to summon the kami (gods) and usher in good fortune.
