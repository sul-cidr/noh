Sanbasō-Encounter:
Mondō 2
Synopsis
Sanbasō calls for an Ado, a term used in Kyōgen to identify a contrasting character. Thus, Senzai appears and encourages Sanbasō to dance as a prayer for blessings to ten thousand years.

Index of Intermedia

The Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this second dan of the second act, consists of a single shōdan, although it raises the IoI, it remains at a low level.

This shōdan features an unaccompanied dialogue between Sanbasō and Senzai.


Following the suspended stillness of Sanbasō's mask-donning, the Sanbasō Mondō opens quietly. As an unaccompanied dialogue, it keeps the Index of Intermedia low, creating a moment of narrative focus before the next major dance. Positioned at Square 1, the now-masked Sanbasō looks left and right, calling for the Ado Tayū, the leader of a performing troupe. Senzai responds from Square 5. What follows is not a polite exchange but a ritualized quarrel. Senzai formally requests that Sanbasō dance as a prayer for "ten thousand years" of blessings, but the prickly deity counters, commanding Senzai to return to his seat first. Their brief standoff, a contest of wills over who acts first, establishes the deity’s difficult and unpredictable nature. Senzai breaks the impasse by taking the initiative and presenting Sanbasō with the suzu, the sacred bell tree that is central to the ritual dance that follows.
