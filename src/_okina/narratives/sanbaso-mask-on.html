Form
Sanbasō-Kyōgen Performs:
Momidashi → Mominodan → Put the mask on
Synopsis
Sanbasō-to-be character puts the mask on and thus becomes Sanbasō, a deity of festive dance and purification, with strong ties to blessings for fertility and prosperity.

Visuals
Sanbasō-to-be goes up-stage and, assisted by a kōken, puts on his mask, and becomes the personification of Sanbasō.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the first dan of the second act consists of three shōdan, maintaining the IoI at its highest level for most of the section.

The last shōdan of this dan suddenly puts the development of the ritual on hold as Sanbasō puts on the black mask. This suspended moment brings the IoI to its lowest level.


Maintaining discretion, as had been done with Okina, who donned his mask while Senzai was dancing, Sanbasō affixes his mask while kneeling on the left, behind the musicians and facing away from the audience. This mask, known as Kokushiki-jō (黒式尉), is black in color and represents an aged, tough, and unsophisticated character. It is often associated with the common people or farmers, thus contrasting with Okina's more refined, white-faced Hakushiki-jō mask.
