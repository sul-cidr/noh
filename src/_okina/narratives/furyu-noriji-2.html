Form
Furyū-Furyū Performs:
Noriji-2
Synopsis
As the jiutai of kyōgen actors sitting on the bridge sing the einori chant, Pine and Bamboo dance.
Text
Irregular rhythm. The text, set in einori is sung in tsuyogin style by the jiutai.
Music
The Taiko, Ōtsuzumi and Kotsuzumi leader are in strict rhythmic settings.
Visuals
As the jiutai credits the reign for the repeating joyous days, and praying for ten thousand more years, Pine and Bamboo perform mirrored kata.
Index of Intermedia

The Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this fourth and last dan of the Furyū consists of a single shōdan, which substantially raises the IoI.

This shōdan features an ōnori chant performed by Pine and the jiutai, accompanied by the taiko, ōtsuzumi, and lead kotsuzumi in strict rhythmic setting. As the play’s fifth dance-to-text section, it also incorporates stage movement and serves as the highlight of the Furyū, bringing the IoI to the dan’s highest level and the play’s third highest overall.


The kotsuzumi and ōtsuzumi maintain the same static one-honji pattern employed in the entrance music and the issei. On this occasion, however, they are joined by the taiko, which embellishes their repetitive pattern with patterns of its own, primarily focused on the honji's initial four beats.

While Senzai Furyū remains at Square 8, Pine and Bamboo, positioned on Squares 2 and 6, respectively, execute mirrored kata.

The text acknowledges the bond between Pine and Bamboo in the symbolism of ten thousand years of prosperity. It also celebrates a reign at peace and its joyous days. For the most part, the text is well-suited to the onori setting, with one syllable per beat. However, certain words, spread over two beats, are emphasized. Consider these examples:

The word ‘en’, from the sentence “Chitose no matsu no en ni hikarete,” is emphasized since it is rhythmically extended from one to two beats. It refers to the special and mysterious connection between Bamboo and Pine and the thousand-year Pine.
The word ‘miyo’, from the sentence “nao masariyuku miyo nakeba,” is emphasized, since it is rhythmically extended from one to three beats. It refers to the reign or time of the current emperor, which is said to be joyous and at peace for thousands of years.

At times, the text is reinforced with mimetic kata. For instance, upon hearing the jiutai commenting on the bond between Bamboo and Pine with the words “Drawn by a connection to the thousand year,” the two characters perform in sync, as if to illustrate their bonding, a sequence of six kata: Raise arms, Open fan, Rotate to Face front, Circle point, Forward point, and Open-retreat. Then, with the text “raise their sleeves aloft, hold them high,” they swirl their left sleeves and position them overhead.

The text is also reinforced with symbolic kata. Within the context of the ritual Noh play Okina, "kazashi kazashite" alludes to emerging auspicious signs. The significance of this idiom is, in fact, underlined in the chant with an extended duration to two beats on the first vowel ‘ka’. At that moment, Pine and Bamboo, holding their left swirled sleeves above their heads, raise their fans and bring them to their chests. This combination of kata conveys an impression of profound appreciation and faith.
