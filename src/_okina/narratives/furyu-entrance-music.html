Form
Furyū-Furyū Enters:
Issei Music → Issei Chant
Synopsis
The 'Entrance of the Furyū Spirits' dan begins with this entrance music shōdan. Having heard strange sounds, Furyūsenzai moves to a waiting position. The slow, anticipatory music then welcomes two spirits onto the stage: Pine, symbolizing longevity and strength, and Bamboo, symbolizing resilience and prosperity. After entering, they acknowledge Furyūsenzai before turning to face the audience, ready to offer their blessings.
Text
Short spoken text remaining from the nanori overlaps with the beginning of the Issei music.
Music
The ōtsuzumi and kotsuzumi leader are in strict rhythmic setting. The nohkan marks the beginning of the shōdan with a short hishigi tone.
Visuals
After recognizing an unexpected sound, Furyūsenzai pivots to face the bridge, then walks briskly to Square 3 and waits. Two masked kyōgen actors costumed and crowned as spirits of Pine and Bamboo enter and take their positions on the bridge at the First and Second Pines. They perform a key gesture, acknowledging Furyūsenzai by pointing their closed fans toward him at 45’10”, before sliding back slightly to face the audience directly.

This five-honji instrumental entrance music has a low Index of Intermedia. It is initiated by a single short high note played by nohkan player and features only the ōtsuzumi and kotsuzumi. The sparse sound texture builds anticipation for the arrival of the spirits of Pine and Bamboo but the music flows lightly, setting a mood characteristic of kyōgen. This lightness is reflected in the simple gait of the arriving spirits, whose supernatural identity is presented with elaborate costumes rather than dramatic weight. The percussion performs a pattern reminiscent of a mitsu-ji: the ōtsuzumi's single stroke in the first half of the measure is answered by the kotsuzumi's three strokes in the second half. This very standard call-and-response between the two drums creates here a very appropraite feeling of waiting and of something approaching from a distance, perfectly matching Furyūsenzai’s expectant posture.
