Synopsis
This is the third subsection of Okina’s fifth section. This is an unaccompanied song prayer from Okina to the Gods.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina.
Music
N/A
Visuals
Okina standing on Square 8, sings the prayer with lowered arms.

This prayer, like the preceding one, is delivered unaccompanied, with Okina standing centerstage on Square 9. After opening and lowering his arms, he recites a prayer that invokes peace and references Manzairaku, as well as an ancient tortoise bearing Heaven, Earth, and Man upon its back.

Manzairaku refers to a significant piece of gagaku and its associated dance, both of which are performed for celebratory occasions. The name translates to "Ten Thousand Years of Happiness" or "Eternal Peace."

Within Japanese culture, the symbolism of the tortoise carrying these three elements is as follows:

The Tortoise:
As a prominent symbol of longevity, wisdom, and good fortune in Japan, the tortoise (specifically the mythological minogame) represents stability, support, and the ancient foundations of the world.
Heaven (天):
Represented by the tortoise's domed upper shell, Heaven refers to the celestial realm of cosmic influence and timing.
Earth (地):
Represented by the tortoise's flat underside, Earth symbolizes the physical world, the land, and one's environment.
Man (人):
Man refers to humanity itself—the actions, choices, and awareness of an individual. Situated between Heaven and Earth, the human sphere is where fate (Heaven) and environment (Earth) intersect with personal decisions to shape one's destiny.

In this cosmology, human destiny is not predetermined; rather, it is a dynamic interaction among these three elements.

The ensuing dance can be divided into three sections, with the first and third sections comprising brief dances set to text, while the longer second section is purely instrumental.
