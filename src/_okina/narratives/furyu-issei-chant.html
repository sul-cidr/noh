Form
Furyū-Furyū Enters:
Issei Music → Issei Chant
Synopsis
The spirits of Pine and Bamboo begin their chant, offering a blessing for the long and proper continuation of the reign. They affirm their own symbolic power, speaking of bamboo lasting a thousand years and pine ten thousand. As they sing, Furyūsenzai shows his engagement by moving steadily closer, drawn in by their auspicious words.
Text
Poetic text in regular rhythm set in einori is sung in tsuyogin style by two kyōgen actors – the spirits of Pine and Bamboo.
Music
The Ōtsuzumi and Kotsuzumi leader are in strict rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The spirits of Pine and Bamboo remain stationary on the bridge at the First and Second Pines, facing the audience as they deliver their chant. In contrast, Furyūsenzai moves gradually closer to the spirits, employing a more realistic, improvised kyōgen style of walking that stands out in contrast to the formality of the spirits.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the ‘Furyū Enters’ dan, which comprises two shōdan, rises to the low mid-level.

The second shōdan features an einori chant performed by Bamboo and Pine supported by the ōtsuzumi and lead kotsuzumi in strict rhythmic setting. This produces a modest increase in the IoI, which nonetheless remains in the low mid-level.


This brief, accompanied chant has a middle-range Index of Intermedia, as the voices of the two spirits join the ōtsuzumi and kotsuzumi continuing their steady pattern in congruent rhythmic setting. The vocal texture becomes richer with two singers chanting their auspicious message in tsuyogin style, but the steady flow retains the relatively simple spirit. An intermedia contrast occurs visually: the formal, static posture of the two spirits, who deliver their chant while facing the audience, is set against the loose, improvised gait of Furyūsenzai, who approaches them with a comparatively realistic kyōgen style of walking. This juxtaposition highlights the different natures of the characters—the divine stillness of the spirits and the engaged, worldly curiosity of the human.
