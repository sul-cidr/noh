Form
Furyū-Furyūsenzai Enters:
Watari byōshi → Noriji-1 → Nanori
Synopsis
Senzai Furyū confirms that the reign is at peace.
Text
Irregular rhythm. The text, set in einori is sung in yowagin style by Senzai Furyū.
Music
The Taiko, Ōtsuzumi and Kotsuzumi leader are in strict rhythmic settings.
Visuals
Senzai Furyū stands on Square 1 to deliver his comforting words.

The rhythmic ostinato introduced in the Watari-byōshi is carried over into the brief noriji-1, an eight-honji section. Standing at Square 1, Senzai Furyū delivers a text that conveys happiness, affirming that “The reign is at peace, waves and wind, quiet!” and “Flowers to adorn our hair are in bloom.”

The reference, in the former statement, to waves and wind serves as a metaphor for tribulations, which are stated to be quiescent. As for the latter statement, "blooming flowers" is an expression employed to celebrate the arrival of a special moment, symbolizing a joyful and propitious occasion.

The text’s rhythmic setting aligns effectively with the noriji structure, wherein lines commence on the second beat or its second half, predominantly with one syllable per beat. On occasion, the rhythm of the text is decelerated from quarter notes to dotted quarter notes, thereby accentuating the corresponding text. For instance, the decelerated rhythm in the second and third honji 42’38” foregrounds the text: “Auspicious indeed! The reign is at peace.” Similarly, for the final two honji of the section 43’17”, the slower-paced text referring to flowers to adorn our hair conveys that these flowers “...are in bloom! Indeed, it is so!”, concluding the section with a sense of faith in the prevailing safety and felicity.
