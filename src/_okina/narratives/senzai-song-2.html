Form
Okina (White Mask)-Senzai Performs:
Senzai Song 1 → Senzai-no-mai 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai-no-mai 2
Synopsis
Through celebratory song and energetic kata, Senzai expresses wishes for a thousand autumns of peace and prosperity. He introduces the metaphor of booming water that retains its power even under the shining sun. This imagery is echoed by the jiutai, and as they sing, Senzai performs a powerful kata that physically embodies the resilience and force of the water.
Text
Regular. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Senzai and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in a strict rhythmic setting.
Visuals
Senzai dances energetically while singing. He performs most of his initial kata upstage on Square 8, but concludes Song 2 by circling the stage and moving up front to Square 4, in preparation for the Dance 2.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this third dan, which comprises four shōdan, rises to the first act’s highest level in a sawtooth pattern.

The third shōdan features an arhythmic song performed by Senzai and the jiutai, supported by three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting. As the play’s second dance-to-text section, it also incorporates stage movement. This combination brings the IoI back down to an upper mid-range level.


As the nohkan stops playing, the Index of Intermedia for this dance-to-text shōdan, Senzai Song 2, descends to the same level as Senzai Song 1. However, the overall momentum remains high, driven by the fast tempo of the music and energetic stage movement. Senzai’s text repeats from earlier Shiku-no-kamiuta a hope for a millennium of flourishing and resilience, like that of a waterfall. Midway through the song, the jiutai responds, their lines affirming that 'unending water flows on, booming.' At this moment, Senzai repeats the mimetic kata from the first song: he bends down before rising onto his toes, embodying the power and resilience of water.

The kata in this shōdan are fast and powerful but initially contained, with movement concentrated in the upper body through swirling sleeves and pointing gestures. This phase of the movement corresponds to the first two of the song’s six honji. For the remaining four honji, Senzai first circles the stage as he collects his sleeves and then moves to the front at Square 4, propelled by a dramatic shift in the percussion. The three drummers re-introduce a faster pattern of even-duration 'ta' and 'po' sound alternation that we have heard in Shiku-no-kamiuta. This momentum is powerfully reinforced as the drumming tempo more than doubles.
