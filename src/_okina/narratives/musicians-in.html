Form
Okina – Procession:
Watari → Zatsuki-bue
Synopsis
Following the oshirabe, or tuning of the instruments, the Senzai performer, who carries the mask box, proceeds across the bridgeway onto the stage. He is followed by the Okina and Sanbasō performers, who enter the stage unmasked. The transformation from human to deity is enacted only as they ceremonially put on their respective masks, a ritual that takes place on stage. They are followed by the musicians.

Visuals
This is a ritualistic procession that features first and foremost two of the play’s three main characters: Senzai and Okina-to-be.

Senzai, carrying Okina’s mask in a casket, initiates the performance by entering the stage. He takes his position at Square 3, a location signifying the great value and importance of the casket he bears 3’28”.

Following Senzai is an elderly man, who will subsequently don the mask and transform into the deity Okina. He pauses at the center of the stage, Square 9, thereby underscoring his elevated rank 4’24”. As he prepares to execute a ritual in deference to the audience and patrons, the remaining actors and musicians remain seated on the hashigakari bridge, refraining from entering the main stage 5’24”.

Thereafter, the elderly man deliberately performs the sa-yū kata, a sequence of left-and-right movements culminating in a bow. He executes the sa-yū kata by synchronizing the movements of the left arm and foot when turning to the left 4’41”, and mirroring this action on the right side 4’52”. The initial turn to the left is an acknowledgement of the higher-ranking side of the stage. This custom is attributed to the imperial system of ancient China, wherein the Emperor, facing south, deemed the east (his left) the more auspicious direction.

This kata sequence is repeated twice before he bows to the audience. The bow is a fundamental aspect of Japanese etiquette, embodying profound respect 5’31”. It is through this gesture that the audience and patrons are recognized for their presence and patronage. Furthermore, the Noh stage itself is regarded as a unique, and at times sacred, location. Performers commonly enter the stage with a sense of gravitas, and the bow serves to delineate the boundary between the temporal world and the dramatic realm of the play. Upon completion of this ritual, the elderly man relinquishes his position at center stage and kneels at Square 7 5’24”.

Subsequently, Senzai conveys the casket to the elderly man. After bowing to the elderly man 9’57”, Senzai executes a sa-yū kata 10’02” prior to moving to Square 3. At this juncture, the individual who will embody the Sanbasō character proceeds to Square 1, followed by the musicians who then take their places on the stage.
