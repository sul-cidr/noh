Form
Okina – Procession:
Okina-watari → Zatsuki-bue
Synopsis
Following the oshirabe, the performance opens in near-silence with a slow, ritualistic procession led by Senzai, a young boy carrying the sacred mask box, followed by the Okina actor. At center stage Okina performs a solo ritual of deliberate movements and a profound bow to sanctify the space. Senzai then presents the mask box to him, after which the arrival of the Sanbasō actor and the musicians completes the introductory scene.

Visuals
The performance opens with a solemn procession across the hashigakari bridge. Senzai leads, carrying the Okina mask in a box, followed by the elderly Okina-to-be. Taking center stage, the Okina-to-be performs a formal solo ritual of precise movements and deep bows. After Senzai presents the casket to him, the actor who will portray Sanbasō arrives, with the musicians following to complete the opening scene.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this first dan, which comprises two shōdan, remains at its lowest level.

The first shōdan is a procession, inaugurates the ritual, as actors and musicians ceremoniously take their place on stage. There is no music, no text—the unfolding of the ritual is momentarily on hold.


This opening sequence has the lowest Index of Intermedia (IoI), as it unfolds in near-complete silence with no music or text. The action is almost purely visual, establishing a solemn and ritualistic atmosphere. Senzai enters first, slowly crossing the hashigakari with a box containing Okina’s mask. He positions himself at Square 3, a location that underscores the casket’s importance 3’28”. He is followed by Okina, the actor who will later transform into the deity—who pauses at center stage (Square 9) to signify his elevated rank 4’24”. Meanwhile, the remaining performers wait on the bridge, their separation from the stage heightening the focus on the two solitary actors 5’24”.

The Okina actor then performs the sa-yū kata — a sequence of left-right-left movements ending in a bow—to honor the Gods. The gesture, which can be attributed to Shinto practice, serves here on stage to mark the boundary between the everyday world and the dramatic realm of the play. He synchronizes his left arm and foot when turning left 4’41”, then mirrors the action on his right 4’52”. As he lowers himself to bow all of the performers, many of whom are already on the hashigakari awaiting their entrance, lower themselves aas well and sitdown creating a powerful group visual gesture.

Upon completing this ritual, the elderly man moves to Square 7 and kneels 5’24”. In the stark silence, the percussive sound of his knees hitting the stage is the only sound heard. This singular noise punctuates the quiet, lending a powerful physical presence to his character. Prompted by this sound, Senzai brings the mask box to the elderly man. After bowing 9’57”, Senzai performs his own sa-yū kata 10’02” and moves to Square 5. At this point, the performers awaiting entrance raise up, the actor playing Sanbasō proceeds to Square 1, followed by the musicians, who then take their places. Their entrance signals the end of this silent, preparatory phase and the imminent introduction of music and chanting, which will progressively raise the Index of Intermedia.
