Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the first dan of the second act consists of three shōdan, maintaining the IoI at its highest level for most of the section.

The first shōdan features both a spoken part and a dance performed by Sanbasō, accompanied by the ōtsuzumi, and three kotsuzumi in a strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. Sanbasō, who remains seated at Square 1 for the first two-thirds of the shōdan, finally stands up and performs a series of kata on the stage’s left side. Although this combination slightly lowers the IoI, it keeps it within the higher range.


The second act, which focuses on Sanbasō, begins with an elevated IoI and a highly energetic rhythmic pattern. While the three kotsuzumi drums keep a steady four-beat pattern, the ōtsuzumi adds strokes between the beats, creating the most dynamic rhythmic layering in the play so far.

At the start of the section, as if summoned by the nohkan's Hishigi pattern, Sanbasō makes an engaging entrance. Starting from behind the musicians to their left, he moves to Square 3 to perform a Forward point, then takes nine steps back to Square 1, where he remains seated for the first two-thirds of the section.

The Forward point and vigorous backward steps are supported by the music: the nohkan’s momidashi pattern concludes with rapid ‘uta’ sounds as the ōtsuzumi drum adds a series of chon strokes. This combination brings the opening of the section’s seven honji to an exhilarating close.

With its shifting patterns set against the steady beat of the three kotsuzumi, the ōtsuzumi drum takes control of the momentum. A subtly syncopated pattern and the drummer’s ‘iya’ kakegoe prompt Sanbasō to rise to one knee and begin his dance for the final third of the shōdan.

Sanbasō delivers his text while performing a series of kata. His words are less a prayer and more an acknowledgment of the joy and good fortune of the present moment.
