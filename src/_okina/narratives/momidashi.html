Visuals
Sanbasō who kneels at Square 1 for the first two-third of the section, stands up for the last third and opens the left side of the stage, with performing kata on primarily on Squares 1, 2, and 3.

This marks the initial section focused on Sanbasō, and it commences with a rhythmic pattern of considerable energy. While the three kotsuzumi maintain a repeating four-beat pattern:

Figure 16

the ōtsuzumi interjects strokes in between beats, thereby generating the most dynamic rhythmic layering observed thus far in the play.

At the section's commencement, as if summoned by the nohkan's Hishigi pattern, Sanbasō enters the stage in a highly engaging manner. Initially positioned behind, to the left of the musicians 54’31”, he proceeds to Square 3, where he executes a Forward point, followed by nine steps back to Square 1, where he remains seated for the first two-thirds of the section.

The Forward point and the vigorous steps backward are supported by a rapid sequence of ‘uta’ sounds from the conclusion of the nohkan's momidashi pattern, and a series of chon strokes by the ōtsuzumi, bringing the opening of the section’s seven honji to an exhilarating close 54’42”.

Figure 17

By virtue of its shifting patterns juxtaposed against the steady pattern of the three kotsuzumi, it is the ōtsuzumi that assumes control of the momentum. It is its subtly syncopated pattern 55’43” and its ‘iya’ kakegoe that prompts Sanbasō to rise onto his right knee before commencing his dance for the final third of the section:

It is while executing a series of kata that Sanbasō delivers his text. This is not so much a prayer as an acknowledgement that the present moment is delightfully imbued with felicity.
