Okina-Okina Performs:
Dance-1 → Dance-2 → Dance-3
Synopsis
Okina appears as slowly emerging as a deity, before finding his momentum.
Text
N/A
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
An unusual dance with a slow first part followed with a faster second part.

Okina's presence and dance serve to evoke a sense of peace, tranquility, and good fortune. He is considered a harbinger of auspicious events and a protector against malevolent influences.

The dance is structured in three sections, with the first and third incorporating dance performed with accompanying text. The second section, the most extended of the three, is an instrumental dance, which itself is further divided into three parts.

The opening portion of this instrumental dance is particularly striking. Evoking the sense of emergence from a world of deities, time is dramatically slowed, with Okina taking approximately four minutes to traverse the distance from Square 9 to Square 3. Over the course of fifteen honji, the three kotsuzumi’s part and the choreography of kata are meticulously synchronized.

The kotsuzumi's two-honji pattern predominantly emphasizes beats 1, 3, 5, and 7. It is on these same beats that Okina executes a step, bringing a foot forward, raising its toes, and then lowering them, as illustrated in the following figure:

During this segment, the nohkan performs the first three phrases of the Okina no mai, a thirteen-phrase pattern.

Upon reaching Square 3, the nohkan observes a tacet for the duration of one and a half honji 28’18”, during which Okina executes three stamps, seemingly affirming his arrival in the terrestrial realm. This action is underscored by a novel kotsuzumi pattern that differentiates the leader's part from those of his two acolytes:

Figure 10

However, this pronouncement appears to be premature, as the deliberate stepping forward resumes. The nohkan rejoins the ensemble to perform its subsequent four phrases 28’39”, and approximately midway between Squares 3 and 4, Okina reiterates the bowing kata and series of stamps 29’37”. This time, however, the process culminates in a marked increase in tempo, as if transitioning into real-time 30’12”, occurring in quasi-synchronization with the nohkan’s eighth phrase.

At this juncture, the focus shifts entirely to the dance. Okina’s sequence of kata dynamically expands the stage space, while the kotsuzumi revert to their repetitive two-honji pattern, and the nohkan completes its remaining six phrases.

Upon arriving at Square 5, Okina rotates and proceeds to Square 9, where he performs a Medium Zigzag 30’34”. Subsequently, after reaching Square 3 for the third and final part of the dance, he shifts the energy from the lower to the upper body with an Overhead Left Sleeve 30’56”, before concealing his face with his fan. He then releases tension with a return upstage to Square 8, where he resets his sleeves 31’10”. Momentum is then rebuilt by returning to Square 3, where he twirls his left sleeve 31’32”. After reaching Square 5, he resets his sleeves and releases momentum, one last time, with a return upstage to Square 8 31’52”, where he performs the standard closing sequence: Small Zigzag and Scooping point. The dance then concludes with a sequence of three stamps 32’19”.
