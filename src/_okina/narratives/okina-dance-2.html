Synopsis
In this extended instrumental dance, Okina performs a ritual blessing for peace, tranquility, and good fortune. The dance is a physical manifestation of the preceding prayer, symbolically traversing the realms of Heaven, Earth, and Man. Each realm is invoked through a distinct, ceremonial sequence of three stamps, making the dance a powerful embodiment of cosmic harmony and a prayer for the world's stability.

Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The dance unfolds in three parts. It begins with an extremely slow, saccaded walk from center stage (Square 9) that takes nearly four minutes; the walk is composed of distinct, multi-part steps where Okina brings a foot forward, raises his toes, lowers them, and then brings the other foot level, holding each phase as a pose. The section is marked by two ceremonial three-stamp sequences: "Heaven" (Left-Right-Left) at 28’18” and "Earth" (Right-Left-Right) at 29’51”, both preceded by a bow. The second part is a burst of fast, dynamic movement, featuring a Medium Zigzag 30’34”. This energetic section includes a striking moment of contrast: after performing an Overhead Left Sleeve 30’56”, Okina then covers his face with his fan, holding this static, shrouded pose while stepping backward slowly against the rapid music. The dance returns to slowness for the final part, concluding with the standard closing kata and the final "Man" stamp sequence (Left-Right-Left), performed with a distinct, even rhythm.

As an instrumental dance, this extended shōdan has a very high Index of Intermedia, just short of the maximum due to the nohkan playing in a non-congruent rhythmic setting. It is structured in three distinct sections (slow-fast-slow) that showcase a vast dynamic range. The first section begins with an extremely slow momentum (MM = 22), creating a profound sense of venerable dignity. Over fourteen honji, the synchronization is meticulous. On odd beats of the kotsuzumi pattern, Okina executes a multi-part step: bringing one foot forward, raising the toes, lowering them, and then bringing the other foot level. Each phase is held like a pose, creating a profound sense of deliberate, ritualistic motion. The nohkan accompanies with the first phrases of the Okina no mai pattern. At the eighth honji, there is a powerful shift: the nohkan becomes silent, creating a stark focus as Okina performs the "Heaven" stamps in a Left-Right-Left sequence 28’18”. Rejoining at 28’39”, the nohkan’s melody reignites the energy for the slow, saccaded walk that builds toward the "Earth" stamps. This second sequence is performed in a reverse Right-Left-Right order 29’51”.

The momentum shifts dramatically at 30’12”, as the second section begins with a considerable increase in tempo (MM = 110). Here, the kotsuzumi shift to a repetitive, driving two-honji pattern, propelling Okina’s fast-paced choreography. A striking moment of contrast occurs midway through this section: against the rapid percussion, Okina brings his sleeve over his head and then covers his face with his fan, holding a static pose while stepping backward slowly. This creates a powerful juxtaposition of stillness against motion, and significantly, the kotsuzumi accompanies this impressive posture by playing the same rhythmic pattern from the ‘Encounter’ dan, creating another strong thematic link. The final section, beginning at 31’41”, returns to a slow, reflective momentum. The percussion slows, the nohkan plays in its lowest register, and the dance concludes with the "Man" stamps 32'24”. This final sequence returns to the Left-Right-Left pattern but is rhythmically and expressively distinct from the first two. It is played at even intervals (short-short-short) without a preceding bow, bringing the entire dance to a solemn and decisive close.
