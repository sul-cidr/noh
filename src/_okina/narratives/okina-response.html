Form
Okina-Okina Prays:
Okina Prayer 1 → Response to Okina → Okina Prayer 2
Synopsis
In response to Okina's prayer, the jiutai sings a slow, melismatic chant, continuing the sacred atmosphere. During their song, Okina performs a small, contained dance, before concluding with a humble gesture: he bends forward and covers his face with his fan, holding a deeply prayerful pose.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in einori, is sung by the jiutai at the beginning in tsuyogin and later in yowagin style.
Music
The kotsuzumi part performed by the lead player is in flexible, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The visual action is focused entirely on Okina, who remains within Square 8. He performs a small zigzag movement, as if acknowledging the gods from different directions. The shōdan concludes with a distinct visual as Okina bends slightly forward, covers his face with his open fan, and holds the still, prayerful posture.

This two-honji passage is designated as a subsection due to its marked intermedia contrast with the preceding prayer. It raises the Index of Intermedia as it features the return of the percussion and nohkan players, although only the lead kotsuzumi player participates. It is also the first time that we hear the lead kotsuzumi play in a flexible rhythmic setting.

The two-honji response, delivered by the jiutai, is characterized by a slow and introspective quality. This is supported by the nohkan's Sō no Shingon no te pattern; with its main sounds in the instrument’s lower register, the pattern is well-suited to the section’s overall reflective mood.

During this time, Okina executes a small zigzag movement, as if acknowledging the gods from all directions. He concludes the shōdan with the chi-no-tappai kata, or “paying respect to Earth.” This is the second of three such kata in the play that honor the fundamental trinity of Heaven, Earth, and Man. Crucially, the performance of this gesture is linked to a vocal shift: the singing style changes from the strong tsuyogin to the more melodious yowagin. This unique combination of a reflective gesture and softer vocal tone is characteristic of Okina's performance and deepens the scene's introspective quality.
