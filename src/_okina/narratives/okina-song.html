Form
Okina-Introductory Song:
Okina Song
Synopsis
The performance transitions into the second shōdan, sung by the performer who will become Okina and the jiutai. The opening line's meaning is unknown, creating a mysterious and ritualistic tone. The song then continues with a blessing that invokes the tortoise and crane—traditional emblems of longevity. In a call-and-response, the Okina-to-be and chorus repeat the unknown text, but this time set it against a dramatically different accompaniment. A simple, driving drum ostinato accelerates to an extreme tempo, generating powerful energy on an otherwise motionless stage and marking the climax of the shōdan.
Text
Arhythmic text set in sashinori, is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The stage is motionless, as the performer who will become Okina sings his part while seated at Square 7.

The performance transitions from the solo flute of the zatsuki-bue to a full ensemble for Okina’s Song, significantly raising the Index of Intermedia. Its ritualistic character is established by the first lines of text whose meaning is now lost. Theories suggest it may be an archaic shōga (solfege) for the nohkan or onomatopoeia—for instance, the sound "to" is later referenced in "Senzai's Song" as a thundering waterfall. The music builds in layers, beginning with the stark, percussive power of the three kotsuzumi drums palying a pattern in rhythmic unison. After four repetitions at a fast tempo, they deliberately slow, creating an opening for the vocals and the nohkan flute, leading to a call-and-response between the Okina-to-be and the chorus.

The ritual text is then repeated, but its intensity is amplified by the musical accompaniment. The three kotsuzumi establish a simple but driving ostinato that undergoes an extreme acceleration. This doubling of tempo generates immense energy, creating the most dynamic experience of the performance so far. The section is audibly framed, sealed off by piercing Hishigi flute pattern at its opening and conclusion. While the performers remain physically motionless, the stage is sonically activated by the vocal alternation and the intensely accelerating drums.
