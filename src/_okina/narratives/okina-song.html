Okina-Introductory Song:
Okina Song
Synopsis
The performance transitions into a second shōdan. It begins with Okina's Song-1, sung by the Okina-to-be and the jiutai. The opening line is delivered in a lost language, which helps establish a ritualistic tone. The song then continues into a blessing that invokes the tortoise and crane—traditional emblems of longevity and good fortune. The musical ritual continues with an alternation by the Okina-to-be and the jiutai by reiterating the onomatopoeic, ritualistic text but sets it against a dramatically different accompaniment. A simple, driving drum ostinato accelerates to an extreme tempo, creating a powerful sense of dynamic energy on an otherwise motionless stage and marking the climax of the shōdan.
Text
Arhythmic text set in sashinori, is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The stage remains motionless as the Okina-to-be performs his vocal part from a seated position at Square 7.

The performance transitions from the solo nohkan of the zatsuki-bue to a full ensemble with Okina’s Song-1, significantly raising the Index of Intermedia. Its ritualistic character is established clearly by the first line of text, which is sung in a language whose semantic meaning is lost to time—hypothesized to be an ancient song, archaic shōga, or even Sanskrit. This transforms the lyrics into a sequence of pure, onomatopoeic sounds. The sonic ritual is built in layers, beginning with the stark, percussive power of the three kotsuzumi drums playing in rhythmic unison. After four repetitions at a fast tempo, they deliberately slow, creating an opening for the vocals and the nohkan flute. The song then unfolds as a call-and-response between the Okina-to-be and the jiutai. This vocal alternation provides the scene's sole dynamic element, sonically activating the stage while the performers remain physically motionless, with Okina seated throughout.

OKINA SONG 2 OLDOkina's Song-2 maintains the same Index of Intermedia as the core elements remain consistent: the stage is visually static, with the Okina-to-be singing from Square 7 in alternation with the jiutai, and the text reiterates the onomatopoeic, ritualistic quality of the first song. The crucial difference, and the source of the scene's new intensity, lies entirely in the musical accompaniment.

The three kotsuzumi drums establish a simple but driving ostinato (a repeating rhythmic pattern) that provides a transparent and coherent foundation for the vocals. The critical shift lies in the tempo: the drums undergo an extreme acceleration—more than doubling in speed—to generate immense energy on the motionless stage, creating the most dynamic experience of the performance so far. After decelerating to signal the song's cadence, the entire singing subsection is audibly framed. Just as it began with the flute's piercing Hishigi pattern, it concludes with another, sealing off this part of the ritual.
