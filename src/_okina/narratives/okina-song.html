Form
Okina-Introductory Song:
Okina Song
Synopsis
The performance transitions into the second shōdan, sung by the performer who will become Okina and the jiutai. The opening line is an incantation without specific meaning that establishes a mysterious and ritualistic tone. The song then continues with a blessing that invokes the tortoise and crane—traditional emblems of longevity. In a call-and-response, the Okina-to-be and chorus repeat the incantation, but this time set it against a dramatically different accompaniment. A simple, driving drum ostinato accelerates to an extreme tempo, generating powerful energy on an otherwise motionless stage and marking the climax of the shōdan.
Text
Regular text set in sashinori, is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The stage is motionless, as the performer who will become Okina sings his part while seated at Square 7.

The performance transitions from the solo flute of the zatsuki-bue to a full ensemble for Okina Song, significantly raising the Index of Intermedia. Its ritualistic character is established by the opening lines. Their sounds, said to derive from a form of solfege, or onomatopoeia that imitates the sound of percussion or wind instruments, are sung like a spell, without forming words. Interestingly, the same sound "to" is referenced in the immediately following Senzai Song 1 as a thundering waterfall. The music builds in layers, beginning with the percussive power of the three kotsuzumi drums playing in rhythmic unison. After four repetitions at a fast tempo, they deliberately slow, creating an opening for the vocals and the nohkan flute, leading to a call-and-response between the Okina-to-be and the chorus.

The ritual text is then repeated, but its intensity is amplified by the musical accompaniment—a heightened expression that may suggest the shamanistic possession of the character. The three kotsuzumi establish a simple but driving ostinato that undergoes an extreme acceleration. A doubling of tempo generates immense energy, creating the most dynamic experience of the performance so far. The shōdan is audibly framed by the piercing Hishigi flute pattern at both its opening and conclusion. While the performers remain physically motionless, the stage is activated sonically, by the vocal alternation and the intense drums.
