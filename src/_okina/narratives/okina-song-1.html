Synopsis
The performance transitions into a second subsection. It begins with Okina's Song-1, sung by the Okina-to-be and the jiutai. The opening line is delivered in a lost language, which helps establish a ritualistic tone. The song then continues into a blessing that invokes the tortoise and crane—traditional emblems of longevity and good fortune.
Text
Arhythmic text set in sashinori, is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina-to-be and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The stage remains motionless as the Okina-to-be performs his vocal part from a seated position at Square 7.

The performance transitions from the solo nohkan of the zatsuki-bue to a full ensemble with Okina’s Song-1, significantly raising the Index of Intermedia. Its ritualistic character is established clearly by the first line of text, which is sung in a language whose semantic meaning is lost to time—hypothesized to be an ancient song, archaic shōga, or even Sanskrit. This transforms the lyrics into a sequence of pure, onomatopoeic sounds. The sonic ritual is built in layers, beginning with the stark, percussive power of the three kotsuzumi drums playing in rhythmic unison. After four repetitions at a fast tempo, they deliberately slow, creating an opening for the vocals and the nohkan flute. The song then unfolds as a call-and-response between the Okina-to-be and the jiutai. This vocal alternation provides the scene's sole dynamic element, sonically activating the stage while the performers remain physically motionless, with Okina seated throughout.

The song commences at measure 10, at which point the nohkan joins with its Hacchō no yuri pattern, a composition of six continuous phrases. The combined effect of the song and the nohkan part creates a seamless continuum, a distinct layer juxtaposed against the pulsating patterns of the three kotsuzumi.

The percussion instruments introduce three distinct eight-beat patterns. The first pattern is predicated upon two distinct sounds: the ‘ta’ and ‘po’ strokes, with the former employed in the first half of the pattern and the latter in the second half. It comprises a sequence of two-beat strokes articulated on beats 1, 3, 5, and 7, its regularity broken only by a stroke on beat 6.5 that serves as an anacrusis, or pick-up, to beat 7, thereby accentuating it. Thus, the first half contains two strokes, while the second half contains three.

The second pattern is initially introduced in measures 6 and 7, anticipating the entrance of the nohkan and Okina’s melody by four honji. It is readily discernible due to the lead kotsuzumi player performing a pattern slightly divergent from those executed by the other two players.

Furthermore, this pattern is more dynamically complex than the first, featuring strokes on beats 1, 3, and 4 in the first half, and 5, 6, 6.5, and 7 in the second half, for a total of seven strokes compared to the first pattern's five.

This pattern is reiterated at measure 20, four measures prior to the section's conclusion, which is itself identified with this pattern.
