Okina – Procession:
Okina Watari → Zatsuki-bue
Synopsis
An opening nohkan solo as musicians settle in their places.
Text
N/A
Music
A nohkan solo.
Visuals
The musicians and their assistants settle onto the stage to prepare for the subsequent section. Throughout this action and the accompanying nohkan solo, all other performers remain still.

The zatsuki-bue is a brief flute solo, about fifty seconds long, that raises the IoI by one step by introducing expressive and ritualistic music. Although its name simply means "taking seats," its expressive power in this context serves to purify the stage and establish the spirit for the sacred ritual of Okina. After all participants have taken their positions, the nohkan player concludes the solo with the Hishigi pattern 11’44”. This high, penetrating tone pierces the atmosphere, commanding attention and marking the definitive transition from preparation to ritual.
