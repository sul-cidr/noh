Okina – Procession:
Watari → Zatsuki-bue
Synopsis
An opening nohkan solo as musicians settle in their places.

N/A
Music
A nohkan solo.
Visuals
The musicians and their assistants settle onto the stage to prepare for the subsequent section. Throughout this action and the accompanying nohkan solo, all other performers remain still.

The zatsuki-bue, a brief flute solo of approximately fifty seconds, raises the IoI by one step by introducing expressive and ritualistic music. It is traditionally considered to 'purify the world,' thereby establishing the context for the sacred ritual of Okina. Once all participants have assumed their positions on stage, the nohkan player concludes the solo with the Hishigi pattern 11’44”. Its high and penetrating tone functions as a powerful auditory signal. It pierces the atmosphere to command attention, strongly marking the transition from preparation to ritual and its performance.
