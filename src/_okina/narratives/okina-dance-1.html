Form
Okina-Okina Performs:
Okina Prayer 1 → Response to Okina → Okina Prayer 2 → Okina Song 1 → Okina-no-mai → Okina Song 2
Synopsis
Okina begins his three-part dance by posing a rhetorical question about his own ancient identity. The jiutai echoes this question with greater urgency before Okina offers a final, confirming statement.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in einori and sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The dance begins with Okina moving calmly from the upstage (Square 8) to center stage (Square 9), using circling kata as if to present himself from all sides. The first part concludes with an emphatic sequence of three stamps: Left-Right-Left. As the tempo increases, his movements become faster, featuring pivots and a Medium Zigzag at center stage. For the final part, he performs a slow Open Retreat, bringing the dance to a gentle close.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this fifth dan, which comprises six shōdan, rises to the first act’s highest level following a steady decrease.

The fourth shōdan features an arhythmic song performed by Okina and the jiutai, supported by three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. It is the play’s third dance-to-text as it also includes stage movements. This combination helps raise the IoI to a high mid-range level.


After the solo chant of the preceding prayer, this contrasting dance-to-text shōdan, accompanied by kotsuzumi and nohkan, has a very high Index of Intermedia. Through a combination of rhetorical questioning, assertive movement, and symbolic gesture, Okina reveals his and Sanbasō's identities as ancient deities. This is achieved through a three-part structure that creates a distinct energetic wave.

The performance begins with a calm flow as Okina rhetorically questions their identity. He moves from upstage (Square 8) to center stage (Square 9), using deliberate circling kata as if to present himself from all sides. This physical questioning is supported musically by a low nohkan pattern (Sō no ryō) and a significant rhythmic choice in the percussion. The drums play a two-honji pattern starting, which is a variant of the rhythm used repeatedly in the ‘Encounter’ dan. This musical echo creates a thematic correspondence between the two shōdan. This initial section culminates both visually and sonically with a ritualistic sequence of three powerful stamps—Left-Right-Left.

The energy surges in the second part as the jiutai enters to insistently repeat the question. Okina's movements become faster, featuring pivots and a Medium Zigzag at center stage, propelled by an abrupt shift in the percussion to a faster, five-stroke pattern. The momentum is further intensified when the nohkan re-enters with a higher, faster pattern (Sō no roku no ge), driving both the dynamic choreography and the rising urgency of the question.

The energy recedes for the closing part. Okina performs a slow Open Retreat while singing again, rhetorically confirming his identity with extended einori melismas. This gentle conclusion is matched by the music, as the percussion slows and the nohkan concludes its phrase softly, bringing the shōdan to a calm and reflective finish.
