Form
Okina-Okina Performs:
Dance-1 → Dance-2 → Dance-3
Synopsis
Okina begins his three-part dance by posing a rhetorical question about his own ancient identity. The jiutai echoes this question with greater urgency before Okina offers a final, confirming statement. This shōdan introduces the central symbolic action of the dance: a sequence of three stamps—Left-Right-Left—which physically represents the first of the three poles (Heaven) introduced in the preceding prayer.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in einori and sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The dance begins with Okina moving calmly from the upstage (Square 8) to center stage (Square 9), using circling kata as if to present himself from all sides. The first part concludes with an emphatic sequence of three stamps: Left-Right-Left. As the tempo increases, his movements become faster, featuring pivots and a Medium Zigzag at center stage. For the final part, he performs a slow Open Retreat, bringing the dance to a gentle close.

This dance-to-text shōdan has a high Index of Intermedia. Through a combination of questioning text, assertive movement, and symbolic gesture, Okina asserts his identity as an ancient deity. This is achieved through a three-part structure that creates a distinct energetic wave. It begins with a calm flow as Okina sings slowly, supported by a low nohkan pattern (Sō no ryō) and a steady but slow drum tempo. This part culminates with a sequence of three powerful stamps—Left-Right-Left 25’38”—that serves as a key structural and symbolic marker, representing 'Heaven,' the first of the three poles. The energy surges in the second part as the jiutai enters; the nohkan falls silent, and the percussion abruptly shifts to a faster, five-stroke pattern at 25’49”, reminiscent of the driving rhythm from Okina’s Song – 1. The momentum is further intensified when the nohkan re-enters with a higher, faster pattern (Sō no roku no ge) at 25’54”. For the final part, the energy recedes as Okina sings again. The percussion pattern is filtered, the tempo slows around 26’00”, and the nohkan concludes its phrase, bringing the shōdan to a calm and reflective finish.
