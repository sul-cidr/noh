Form
Okina-Okina performs:
Dance-1 → Dance-2 → Dance-3
Synopsis
Okina and the jiutai who speaks for him, appear to be reflecting on the earlier Encounter, as they are still reflecting on who were these two old men.
Text
Arhythmic. The text, set in sashinori is sung in tsuyogin style by Okina and the jiutai.
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
Okina is opening the stage space increasing the momentum in preparation for the coming instrumental dance.

Okina's dance comprises three sections. The first and third are dance-to-text, while the second, and most extended section, is an instrumental dance.

The text, sung in alternation by Okina and the jiutai, poses a question seemingly related to the Encounter section: "Who are these old men?"

Upon singing his initial line, Okina executes a series of kata that expand the stage space, moving from Square 8 to frontstage Square 3, before circling to Square 9. The overall tempo is deliberately slow, at approximately ♩ = 65.

Okina's first line is supported by the nohkan's Sō no ryō pattern. Concurrently, the three kotsuzumi perform a sequence of strokes that decrease in number per honji, from seven in measure 2 24’58” to four in measure 5 25’38”.

Subsequently, Okina performs a series of three stamps – Left-Right-Left – that precedes the entrance of the jiutai. This entry is marked by a significant increase in tempo, reaching approximately ♩ = 140. At this point, the percussion part transitions to a repeated one-honji pattern of five strokes:

It is reminiscent of the percussion first pattern performed in Okina’s Song – 1, with strokes on every second beats: 1, 3, 5, and 7. Here the stroke on beat 2.5 disturbs the pattern’s periodicity, the same way the stroke on beat 6.5 did in the Song – 1 pattern:

Figure 8

Furthermore, in both instances, the only stroke not preceded by a kakegoe is the one following the half-beat stroke.

The momentum is further sustained by the nohkan, which initiates its second pattern, Sō no roku, in measure 7 25’54”. During this period, Okina executes a Medium Zigzag, maintaining his position center stage.

Then, in measure 10, the tempo reverts to ♩ = 65 for Okina’s second, and final, line of text. This line is delivered as he concludes his Medium Zigzag with an Open-retreat. This increase in the number of kata enhances momentum, effectively preparing for the instrumental dance in the subsequent subsection.
