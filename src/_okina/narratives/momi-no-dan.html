This is an instrumental dance performed by Sanbasō-to-be character. It has three main features: stamping, sleeve’s swirling, and a stage motion between squares, 3, 5, and 8.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the first dan of the second act consists of three shōdan, maintaining the IoI at its highest level for most of the section.

The dan’s second shōdan is an instrumental dance, accompanied by the ōtsuzumi and three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting, and a congruent nohkan part. As the fourth of five instrumental dances in Okina, it temporarily forgoes verbal expression in favor of choreographic refinement, thereby raising the IoI to its peak.


The Momi no dan dance intricately interweaves choreography with music, constituting a highly ritualized and auspicious performance. It is intended to purify the stage, ward off malevolent influences, and bestow blessings of prosperity and longevity upon the audience.

Throughout these seven sections, Sanbasō-to-be transitions from Square 8 to 3, to 5, and back to 8 a total of eleven times. It alternates between extended journeys spanning approximately 12 honji, shorter ones spanning approximately 4 honji, and medium-sized ones spanning approximately 8 honji.

The two primary kata employed in the dance relate to stamping, followed by a step forward or backward, and sleeve swirling. The initial motion (Square: 8-3-5-8) is executed with a sequence of stamps followed by a step forward, spanning 12 honji. The second and third motions involve swirling both sleeves, with the former spanning 6 honji and the latter spanning 3.5 honji. The fourth motion involves walking, confined to Squares 8 – 5 - 8, spanning 3.5 honji. The fifth motion involves stamps and a step forward, swirling both sleeves in the process, progressing from Square 8 – 5 – 3- 8, spanning 11.5 honji. The sixth motion involves walking, with only the left sleeve reset, confined to Squares 8 – 3 – 8, spanning 4.5 honji. The seventh motion involves stamps and a step forward, with both sleeves swirled, spanning 12 honji. The eighth motion involves walking with both sleeves swirled, confined to Squares 8 – 5 - 8, spanning 3.5 honji. The ninth motion involves a sequence of stamps followed by a step forward, resetting and re-swirling the left sleeve, progressing from Square 8 – 5 – 3- 8, spanning 11.5 honji.The tenth motion involves walking, spanning 7 honji.The eleventh and final motion involves stamps and a step forward, with both sleeves reset before being swirled again, only to be reset at the very end, spanning 9 honji.

The dynamic sequences of stamps are often supported by Sanbasō's kakegoe ‘iya’ on beat 8.5 and ‘ho’ on beat 4.5. Conversely, the quieter walking motions (Square 8-3-5-8) are usually performed without any kakegoe.

The kotsuzumi's pattern is a one-honji pattern, initially introduced for Okina's song 1, striking every second beat, with each beat preceded by a kakegoe: iya for the first beat and ho for the third and fifth. The seventh beat is preceded by a po stroke rather than a kakegoe. As for the ōtsuzumi's part, while its function in the Momidashi was to interact with the kotsuzumi's steady pattern, here the focus is on the dance; therefore, it also has a steady pattern with chon strokes on most even beats: 1, 2, 4, and 6.
