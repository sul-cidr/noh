Form
Okina-Senzai performs:
Song-1 → Dance-1 → Song-2 → Dance-2
Synopsis
This is Okina’s first instrumental dance.
Text
N/A
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
This is a very dynamic dance. Besides retreats to Square 8, it is performed primarily on the two strongest squares: 4 and 9. Moreover, the kata involves, in a short span of time, upper body motions accentuated with sleeves swirling, released with pointing gestures, and lower body motions with stamping.

Senzai embodies the continuity of tradition, receiving the wisdom of Okina and representing the hope for a prosperous future. He is also representative of the audience. Like Okina, Senzai's role is to invoke blessings and good fortune, his energetic performance designed to inspire and uplift the audience. Following the opening of the stage space during Song-1, the dance commences with arm motions performed on the dynamic Square 4. Tension is then released with a return to the upstage Square 8, where a right stamp ±16'13" marks the formal beginning of the dance. Although remaining on Square 8, Senzai sustains momentum through arm motions that include swirling and resetting of the sleeves.

A large zigzag movement takes the dancer from upstage to frontstage, occupying Square 5, then Square 3, before returning to Square 5. Here, Senzai performs another series of arm motions and sleeve swirling. He concludes this first section with a return to Square 8.

Following a large right circle that returns him to Square 8, Senzai executes a sequence of pointed, swirling sleeves, moving him to Square 9. He resets his sleeves by swinging his arms downward. A subsequent large left circle concludes the second part of the dance, bringing Senzai back to Square 8. After a two-handed front point, he concludes the dance with a sequence of left-right foot stamps, the final stamp marking the beginning of Song-2.

The nohkan supports the dance with a sequence of six phrases. The first phrase, opening with the 'O-hya' pattern, lasts for three honji, aligning with the dance's three honji-long introduction. However, the remaining five nohkan phrases do not align with the dance, creating a musical continuum independent from it and from the percussion. The percussion pattern, seemingly unaffected by the transition from song to dance, maintains the same three-beat pattern that began in Song-1.
