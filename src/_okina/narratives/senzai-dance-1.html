Okina-Senzai Performs:
Senzai Song 1 → Senzai-no-mai 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai-no-mai 2
Synopsis
Following his song, Senzai performs an energetic dance that translates the theme of flowing water into pure movement. His performance, meant to invoke blessings, uses dynamic arm motions and swirling sleeves as he moves in large patterns across the stage. The dance concludes with a sequence of foot stamps, which provide a powerful transition into the next shōdan.

Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this third dan, which comprises four shōdan, rises to the first act’s highest level in a sawtooth pattern.

The second shōdan is an instrumental dance supported by three kotsuzumi drums in a strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan part. This is the first of five instrumental dances in Okina, during which the elegance and beauty of stage motion momentarily replace verbal expression, thus elevating the IoI to its highest level.


As an instrumental dance, this shōdan has a very high Index of Intermedia. Like Okina, Senzai's role is to invoke blessings, and his energetic performance is meant to uplift the audience. The dance formally begins with a right stamp at Square 8 16’13”. From there, Senzai moves across the stage in a series of large zigzag and circular patterns, using dynamic arm motions and swirling sleeves to sustain momentum. To conclude the dance, he performs a sequence of right-left foot stamps 17’35”, ending with a final right stamp that serves as a smooth transition into Song-2.

The music provides layered support. The nohkan pattern, called senzai no mai, consists of four phrases. While the first aligns with the dance's introduction, the other three create a musical continuum independent of the dance and percussion. Its melody represents water flowing downward; the first note of each successive phrase descends in pitch, from the high 'o-hya' of the first phrase to the middle 'hi-yaaaa', the low 'hi-uuu', and finally the lowest 'ho-hya' of the fourth. Insistent, strongly-attacked, and extended notes further evoke the incessant and powerful flow of water. Meanwhile, the percussion is unaffected by the transition from song to dance. It maintains the same simple, repetitive short-long rhythm from Song-1, creating a seamless sense of continuity between the two shōdan.
