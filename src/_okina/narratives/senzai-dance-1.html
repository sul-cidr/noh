Form
Okina-Senzai performs:
Song-1 → Dance-1 → Song-2 → Dance-2
Synopsis
This is Okina’s first instrumental dance.
Text
N/A
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
This is a short dynamic dance. In a short span of time it involves, upper body motions accentuated with sleeves swirling, released with pointing gestures, and lower body motions with stamping.

Like Okina, Senzai's role is to invoke blessings. His energetic performance is meant to inspire the audience and represents a high Index of Intermedia. The dance formally begins with a right stamp at Square 8 16’13”. From there, Senzai moves across the stage in a series of large zigzag and circular patterns, using dynamic arm motions and swirling sleeves to sustain momentum. To conclude the dance, he performs a sequence of right-left foot stamps, ending with a final right stamp that marks the beginning of Song-2.

The music provides layered support. The nohkan plays four phrases; while the first aligns with the dance's introduction, the other three create a musical continuum independent of the dance and percussion. Its melody seems to represent water flowing downward, with patterns starting first on high pitches and ending with lower ones. Insistent, strongly-attacked notes further evoke the continuous and powerful flow of water. Meanwhile, the percussion is unaffected by the transition from song to dance. It maintains the same simple, repetitive short-long rhythm from Song-1, creating a seamless sense of continuity between the two shōdan.
