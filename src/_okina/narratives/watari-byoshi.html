Form
Furyū-Furyūsenzai Enters:
Watari-byōshi → Noriji-1 → Nanori
Synopsis
The 'Furyūsenzai Enters' dan begins with the Watari-byōshi, a brief entrance music shōdan that brings the character Furyūsenzai, a kyōgen actor, onto the stage. As an unexpected visitor, he arrives to aid the celebration by summoning auspicious spirits.
Text
N/A
Music
The taiko, ōtsuzumi and the leader of the kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The visual action is a standard Noh entrance performed by Furyūsenzai. He enters the stage toward the end of the third honji and reaches Square 1 by the end of the fifth. Once there, he performs a Forward Point to Square 2 before immediately returning to Square 1, where he awaits the next part of the performance.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of the ‘Furyūsenzai Enters’ dan, which comprises three shōdan, rises across its first two shōdan before dropping sharply in its last.

The first shōdan marks the first appearance of the taiko. It features an entrance music for taiko, ōtsuzumi, the lead kotsuzumi player in strict rhythmic setting, and a non-congruent nohkan part. It brings the IoI to a low-medium level, introducing Furyūsenzai as an unexpected visitor to the celebration.


As the first shōdan of the 'Furyūsenzai Enters' dan, this concise entrance music has a medium Index of Intermedia, characterized by the full participation of the hayashi and congruent setting of the nohkan. For the first time, the taiko drum joins the ensemble, adding its weight to the watari-byōshi rhythmic pattern performed by the percussion. The nohkan reinforces this tight, rhythmic feel by playing a simple four-phrase pattern rhythmically locked directly with the drums. It is against this insistent, unified beat that FuryūSenzai, an unexpected visitor summoning auspicious spirits, makes his physical entrance: he walks on toward the end of the third honji and reaches Square 1 by the end of the fifth, where his decisive Forward Point to Square 2 and immediate return echo the same directness as the music. Jointly, sound and movement create a straightforward and joyful atmosphere befitting a kyōgen performance. The unusual richness of this musical setting for a kyōgen interlude clearly signals that this is a performance for a special celebratory occasion.
