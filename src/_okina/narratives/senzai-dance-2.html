Form
Okina-Senzai Performs:
Senzai Song 1 → Senzai-no-mai 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai-no-mai 2
Synopsis
Senzai launches into his final, energetic dance with a striking sequence of seven rapidly executed stamps. As he performs, the most significant event of the dan unfolds. In the background, the Okina-to-be discreetly puts on the white Hakushiki-jō mask, completing his ritual transformation into the deity Okina 18’42”. The understated nature of this pivotal moment emphasizes its solemn, ritualistic power over dramatic display, marking a shift from human preparation to divine presence.

Visuals
The dance begins with a striking sequence of seven rapidly exectued stamps. The choreography is faster and more dynamic than the first dance, incorporating a large zigzag movement, swirling sleeves, and, for the first time, the use of an open fan held at arm's length. After a final large circle, the dance concludes with a right foot stamp.
Index of Intermedia

The overall Index of Intermedia (IoI) of this third dan, which comprises four shōdan, rises to the first act’s highest level in a sawtooth pattern.

The dan’s fourth and final shōdan is an instrumental dance, accompanied by three kotsuzumi drums in strict rhythmic setting and a non-congruent nohkan. As the second of five instrumental dances in Okina, it temporarily forgoes verbal expression in favor of choreographic refinement, returning the IoI to its peak.


This second instrumental dance marks the expressive climax of the dan, and its high Index of Intermedia is also marked by the return of the nohkan. While the sequence of kata is almost identical to that of Senzai-no-mai 1, its expressive character is completely transformed by the intermedia context. The choreography is performed much faster, with the addition of a sequence of seven emphatic stamps and the use of an open fan. While this energetic dance unfolds, the most significant ritual action occurs: the Okina-to-be dons the white mask, transforming into the deity. This pivotal moment happens discreetly, without specific musical or choreographic highlighting, emphasizing its nature as a solemn ritual rather than a dramatic performance.

The musical layers are what drive this transformation. The percussion shifts from the three-beat pattern of Senza-no-mai 1 to a driving two-beat rhythm carried over from the end of Senzai Song 2, powerfully accelerating the momentum. Meanwhile, the nohkan attempts to play the same Senzai-no-mai pattern from the first dance, but because the choreography is now so fast, it only completes the first two phrases. This creates a notable tension between the dance's accelerated pace and the nohkan's independent timeline. The shōdan concludes decisively with the nohkan's piercing Hishigi pattern, a powerful auditory signal that provides formal closure to both the dance and the entire Senzai dan.
