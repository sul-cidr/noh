Senzai Song 1 → Senzai Dance 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai Dance 2
Synopsis
As Senzai performs his final, energetic dance, the most significant event of the dan unfolds. In the background, the Okina-to-be discreetly puts on the white Hakushiki-jō mask, completing his ritual transformation into the deity Okina 18’42”. The understated nature of this pivotal moment emphasizes its solemn, ritualistic power over dramatic display, marking a shift from human preparation to divine presence.

Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
The dance begins with a striking sequence of seven rapidly exectued stamps. The choreography is faster and more dynamic than the first dance, incorporating a large zigzag movement, swirling sleeves, and, for the first time, the use of an open fan held at arm's length. After a final large circle, the dance concludes with a right foot stamp.

This dance marks the expressive climax of the dan, and its high Index of Intermedia is signaled by the return of the nohkan. The choreography is faster and more dynamic than the first dance, beginning formally with an emphatic sequence of seven stamps at Square 8 18’20”. From there, Senzai’s performance incorporates a large zigzag movement 18’28”, swirling sleeves 18’35”, and the use of an open fan. While this energetic dance unfolds, the most significant ritual action occurs: the Okina-to-be discreetly dons the white mask, transforming into the deity 18’42”. This pivotal moment happens without specific musical or choreographic highlighting, emphasizing its nature as a solemn ritual rather than a dramatic performance.

The musical layers reinforce the dance’s climactic energy while providing structural continuity. The percussion carries over the accelerated, even-duration rhythm from the end of Song-2, seamlessly binding the two shōdan together. The nohkan rejoins the ensemble, playing patterns identical to the first two phrases from Dance-1, which creates a sense of symmetry within the dan. The shōdan concludes decisively with the nohkan's piercing Hishigi pattern, a powerful auditory signal that provides formal closure to both the dance and the entire Senzai dan.
