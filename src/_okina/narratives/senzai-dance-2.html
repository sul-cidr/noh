Form
Okina-Senzai Performs:
Senzai Song 1 → Senzai Dance 1 → Senzai Song 2 → Senzai Dance 2
Synopsis
This is Okina’s second instrumental dance.
Text
N/A
Music
The three kotsuzumi are in strict, and the nohkan in non-congruent rhythmic settings.
Visuals
Dance-2 is essentially a variation on Dance-1. The structure and use of the stage space is the same except for some kata.

At the commencement of Dance-2, Senzai occupies the prominent Square 4. Following a sequence of arm motions and a step back, he returns, after two measures, to Square 8, where he initiates the formal choreography with a sequence of seven stamps 18'20". After executing arm kata that incorporate the sleeves, he performs a large zigzag movement 18'28" that brings him frontstage to Square 5. Following a swirling motion of the left sleeve 18'35", he opens his fan and holds it with an extended arm, continuing to occupy the frontstage with a movement to Square 3. After a left point, he returns to Square 5, and after resetting his sleeve, proceeds to Square 8 18'52", where he moves his fan to the left hand before performing a large circle that returns him to Square 8.

He opens the stage space for a final time, executing a double swirling motion of the sleeves and resetting them before concluding the dance with a right foot stamp.

The return of the nohkan contributes to an increased IoI in this section, mirroring its effect in Dance-1. Its patterns are identical to those performed in Dance-1, with the exception of the sixth pattern, which is replaced by the strident Hishigi pattern. The percussion pattern performed throughout Dance-2 remains consistent with the pattern that concluded Song-2.

It is during this dance that the Okina-to-be unobtrusively dons the white mask, known as Hakushiki-jō (白式尉), which translates as "white-robed old man". White is a color of utmost reverence and purity in Japan thus the white mask represents a sacred and benevolent deity or spirit. By donning the mask, the Okina-to-be transforms into Okina, the deity 18'42". It is notable that such a significant moment occurs without drawing attention, suggesting that the action is not a dramatic performance but a solemn, naturalistic, and essential ritual of transformation from a human being into a sacred deity.

This being said, Okina is not a clearly defined god with a specific domain in the manner of deities in Greek or Roman mythology. He is more accurately described as a divine manifestation or an emissary of the divine. He represents the culmination of age, experience, and accumulated wisdom, embodying a connection to the ancestral past and serving as a source of stability and tradition.
