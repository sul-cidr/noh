Shōdan in Okina

Okina-no-mai

Percussion
Strict
Nohkan
Non-congruent

The dance is divided in three parts, each articulated by a cadential sequence of three stamps: left-right-left. The sequences are respectively named: Heaven, Earth, and Man reflecting the Eastern concept of a trinity that symbolizes the completeness of the universe. This fundamental trinity was first referenced in Prayer-2, and similarly named appeared in Okina’s second Dance-to-text.

The percussion part is based on essentially four patterns, while the nohkan’s “Okina no mai” pattern comprises thirteen phrases. The form of the dance is outlined in the chart below, which presents the number of honji that corresponds to the nohkan phrases, percussion pattern, and the title for each sequence of stamps.

Each phrase of the nohkan begins in a distinct register, forming “registral waves”: Part I ascends from low to high; Part II moves from middle to low before ascending to high; and Part III, beginning in the low register, rises to high, then shifts through the middle and low, ultimately concluding in the very low register.
