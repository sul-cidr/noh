Shōdan in Okina

Okina-gaeri

Percussion
Strict

In the final dan, Exit, the Okina actor removes his mask and walks the bridge in a ritualistic mode, with a percussion accompaniment of mystical resonance. This special quality and importance of the character is why this exit section has traditionally been given a special name, Okina-gaeri. The Okina actor proceeds to Square 4, where he performs a reversed sayū kata, raising and pivoting three times—left, right, left—rather than in the standard order of right, left, right. He then steps back to Square 9, where he kneels and bows.

Finally, Okina exits the stage while the three kotsuzumi players perform a single eight-beat pattern, focusing like many preceding patterns on beats: 1, 3, 5, and 7:

{% include image-no-background.html src="/assets/images/Gaeri-1.png" %}

As this eight-beat pattern repeats and the Okina actor almost reaches the first pine on the bridge, the nohkan plays a short passage named yorokobi-no-te, namely “pattern of joy.” This passage works as a releaser for the kotsuzumi players to move on to the next pattern, and for the Okina actor to quicken his gait.

Together with the rising tempo of the actor’s steps, the rhythmic pattern is accelerated to a four-beat pattern, with each stroke on an odd beat preceded by an anacrusis:
