Shōdan in Okina

Watari-byōshi

Percussion
Strict
Nohkan
Congruent

The Watari-byōshi shōdan introduces entrance music commonly used in Noh plays, most often for the entrance of non-human characters. There is also a Kyōgen version, which is much shorter than the Noh version. In this recording, it consists of only seven honji and is performed in a lighthearted, playful manner suited for a Kyōgen interlude. The taiko repeats a commonly used basic pattern called koi-ai, while the ōtsuzumi and lead kotsuzumi perform the mitsu-ji pattern in strict mode, as illustrated in the figure below.

{% include image-no-background.html src="/assets/images/Wataribyoshi.png" %}

The nohkan’s congruent pattern comprises four phrases, exhibiting an A–B–A–C formal structure. Each phrase, itself composed of three patterns spanning the length of an honji and a half, begins with the same shōga: Hiu-ya ra.
